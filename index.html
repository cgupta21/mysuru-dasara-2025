Festival Overview

Experience the grandeur of Karnataka's most celebrated heritage festival

Historical Legacy

Mysuru Dasara celebrates the victory of Goddess Chamundeshwari over the demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil. This 416th consecutive celebration has been celebrated since the 14th century Vijayanagara Empire, with over 400 years of unbroken royal patronage since 1610.

Cultural Heritage

As Karnataka's state festival (Nadahabba), Dasara showcases the rich cultural traditions, classical arts, folk performances, and royal heritage. The festival represents a unique blend of spiritual devotion, royal tradition, and cultural celebration that defines Karnataka's identity.

Royal Traditions

Under the patronage of Yaduveer Krishnadatta Chamaraja Wadiyar, the current royal family continues centuries-old traditions including private Dasara rituals, royal durbar sessions, and symbolic court gatherings reminiscent of ancient ruler meetings.

Special Events & Sacred Ceremonies

Sacred rituals and grand celebrations marking the festival's spiritual significance

Ayudha Puja - Sacred Worship of Instruments

October 1, 2025 (Maha Navami) Also known as Shastra Puja or Astra Puja

🕉️ Sacred Timing

Vijaya Muhurat: 2:02 PM to 2:50 PM
(Most auspicious 48-minute window)

Navami Tithi: September 30, 6:06 PM to October 1, 7:01 PM

👑 Royal Palace Ceremonies

6:00 AM Chandi Homa ritual begins inside palace
6:45 AM Royal elephant, horse, cow enter through Elephant Gate
7:30-7:42 AM 32 royal weapons ceremoniously taken to Kodi Someshwara Temple
Morning Yaduveer Wadiyar performs Ayudha Puja at Kalyana Mantapa

⚔️ Sacred Royal Weapons

32 traditional weapons representing the arms of Goddess Durga's 18 hands:

  • Royal sword from Vijayanagara period
  • Vijaya Narasimha sword
  • Ranadhira Kantheerava sword
  • Krishnaraja Wadiyar's weapon
  • 28 other traditional weapons of divine significance

📿 Spiritual Significance

Historical Origins: Ancient warrior tradition of worshipping weapons before battle, now modernized to honor all tools that support livelihood.

Divine Connection: Celebrates gratitude for instruments that enable daily life and work, symbolizing respect for knowledge, prosperity, and divine power.

Three Goddesses: Worship of Saraswati (knowledge), Lakshmi (wealth), and Parvati/Durga (strength).

🎯 Public Rituals & Customs

  • Thorough cleaning and decoration of all tools, vehicles, instruments
  • Adornment with turmeric paste, sandalwood, kumkum, flower garlands
  • Mango leaves and banana saplings used for decoration
  • White pumpkin smashing ritual in front of vehicles for protection
  • Students worship books and learning instruments seeking knowledge blessings
  • Craftsmen honor their trade tools, professionals worship work instruments
  • Farmers celebrate agricultural implements, mechanics honor their tools
  • Kalash Puja with sacred water pot decorated with mango leaves and coconut

🙏 Religious Ceremonies

Sacred Mantras: Special Vedic chanting and traditional prayers

Offerings: Fruits, flowers, sweets, special naivedyam to blessed instruments

Community Worship: Family gatherings and community prayers celebrating work and prosperity

Symbolic Message: Ordinary instruments become sacred through skill, integrity, and divine blessing
